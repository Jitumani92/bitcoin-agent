Bitcoin risks new 'purge' with bear-market losses still $35B below 2022 total

📰 Cointelegraph.com News 📅 June 08, 2026 Original Source →

The latest data shows that Bitcoin's realized losses, which represent the aggregate value at which investors have purchased their coins but not yet sold them at a loss, remained below the $211 billion recorded in 2022. This indicates that many investors are still holding onto their Bitcoin, despite the significant drops in its value this year.

For Bitcoin and its investors, this situation matters because it could signal that the cryptocurrency's bear market bottom has not yet been reached. Realized losses can serve as a barometer for market sentiment, and when they are high, it often indicates that investors have sold off their holdings at a loss, suggesting capitulation and potential market bottoming out. However, if realized losses remain low, as is currently the case with Bitcoin, it may indicate that there is still room for further price declines.

For investors, this means that the sell-off in Bitcoin could potentially continue, putting downward pressure on its price. While this could represent an opportunity to buy at lower prices, it also carries the risk of further losses. Investors should be cautious and consider their risk tolerance before making decisions regarding their Bitcoin holdings.

💡 Key Takeaway

The continued low realized losses for Bitcoin suggest that the next bear market bottom may not have been reached yet, potentially putting downward pressure on its price.

Bitcoin bears face $2.6B trap as BTC funding rate drops: Is a short squeeze brewing?

📰 Cointelegraph.com News 📅 June 08, 2026 Original Source →

In recent days, Bitcoin's (BTC) price has dipped below the $60,000 mark, leading a surge in short positions among investors betting on further price declines. However, this massive short leverage of approximately $2.6 billion might be setting up a perfect condition for an upside squeeze, as evidenced by the drop in Bitcoin's funding rate – a fee that long and short traders pay or receive to incentivize balance in the market.

The bearish sentiment among investors, driven by regulatory concerns and profit-taking from the recent all-time high of $64,800, has led many traders to open short positions. With such an extensive amount of capital on the line, a sudden shift in market sentiment could lead to a sharp increase in Bitcoin's price, causing those with short positions to buy back the cryptocurrency to cut their losses – a phenomenon known as a "short squeeze."

Such a development would not only result in a substantial price increase for BTC but also pose potential profits for long-term investors who have remained confident during this period of market turbulence. In essence, the $2.6 billion in short leverage could become a self-fulfilling prophecy, pushing Bitcoin's price upward and potentially leading to significant gains for those who stayed the course.

💡 Key Takeaway

A potential short squeeze is brewing in the Bitcoin market, as bears have piled on $2.6 billion in short positions at a low funding rate, which could lead to a sudden increase in BTC's price and generate profits for long-term investors.
