7 major Bitcoin mining pools join Stratum V2, working group
📰 Cointelegraph.com News 📅 May 10, 2026
Seven significant Bitcoin mining pools have recently announced their participation in a new version of the Stratum protocol (Stratum V2) and a collaborative workgroup dedicated to improving the efficiency and security of the Bitcoin mining ecosystem. This move is expected to benefit individual miners by providing more transparency, flexibility, and resistance against potential attacks.
The adoption of Stratum V2 by these major mining pools, including F2Pool, Antpool, BTC.com, Poolin, Viabtc, Slush Pool, and Bitclub SV, aims to standardize the communication between miners and pool operators. By doing so, it promises to streamline the mining process, reduce costs, and increase the overall efficiency of Bitcoin mining operations.
💡 By adopting Stratum V2 and forming a workgroup, seven major Bitcoin mining pools are working together to enhance the transparency, flexibility, and security of the Bitcoin mining ecosystem, benefiting individual miners and investors alike.

Strategy CEO Phong Le says company will sell BTC only in specific cases
📰 Cointelegraph.com News 📅 May 10, 2026
Phong Le, CEO of cryptocurrency investment firm Strategy, has clarified that the company's Bitcoin holdings, which account for more than 4% of the digital currency's maximum supply, will not be sold indiscriminately. In a recent interview, Le stated that the company would only liquidate its BTC holdings in specific cases such as to meet redemption requests from investors or to manage risks in their portfolio.
The announcement has allayed concerns among Bitcoin enthusiasts and analysts about potential massive sell-offs by institutional investors, which could destabilize the market. While Strategy's holdings are substantial, Le emphasized that the company's actions will not significantly impact the broader market due to its focus on strategic selling rather than market manipulation.

Story 6
Swiss Bitcoin reserve campaign set to lapse after failing to gather signatures
📰 Cointelegraph.com News 📅 May 10, 2026
A campaign aimed at requiring the Swiss National Bank (SNB) to hold Bitcoin in its reserves has failed to gain enough support, as organizers fell short of collecting the necessary 100,000 signatures by the deadline. The initiative, which began in June 2021, sought to amend the Swiss Constitution to incorporate digital currencies like Bitcoin into the country's central bank reserves.
This failure marks a significant setback for the Bitcoin community and cryptocurrency advocates within Switzerland, as the proposed referendum could have potentially legitimized Bitcoin on a national level. The lack of support highlights lingering skepticism among Swiss citizens about the role of digital currencies in the country's financial system.
💡 The Swiss Bitcoin reserve campaign failed to gather enough signatures, leaving the possibility of Bitcoin being held in the country's central bank reserves uncertain.
